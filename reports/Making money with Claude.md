# Put Claude in the back office, not the storefront

As of late 2026, a Claude subscription makes money when it replaces labor behind something a human sells, such as a niche product line, a client deliverable or a piece of software. It does not make money when it mass-produces generic output for a marketplace to sell for you. Print-on-demand (POD) still works, and Claude fits it well, but only as the research, copywriting, vector-design and automation layer. **Claude does not generate raster images**, so the artwork itself comes from a paid image tool or your own hand. The platforms have made "upload hundreds of AI designs and wait" unprofitable. **Amazon cut Merch on Demand royalties roughly in half on June 1, 2026 for sellers who rely on organic Amazon traffic**, and it now lets shoppers generate their own AI shirts. Etsy requires AI and production-partner disclosure and is growing sellers faster than buyers. Spring shut down on September 15, 2026. Realistic POD income is **$50–300/month for beginners and $1–3k/month for consistent sellers after a year or more**, and those figures come from blogs, not audited data. The strongest large YouTube income claims turn out, on inspection, to come from selling courses, communities and sponsorships about the method. Anthropic's Consumer Terms let you sell what you make with Pro or Max. They do not let you share your login, resell access, or plug your subscription into a product that serves other people. The practical recommendation: start with a narrow, human-directed Etsy + Printify niche that uses Claude for everything except final art direction, and apply to Merch on Demand in parallel. If you can code or sell, treat a Claude Code-powered service business as the higher-expected-value alternative.

## Rugs and mugs come through Amazon's second door, not Merch

Amazon has two separate POD routes, and confusing them is the most common beginner mistake. **Merch on Demand** (formerly Merch by Amazon) is a royalty program. You upload artwork, Amazon prints, ships, handles customer service and pays you a royalty. Its catalog covers t-shirts, hoodies, sweatshirts, tank tops, polos, quarter-zips, phone cases, PopSockets, throw pillows, tote bags, tumblers and water bottles, sold across seven marketplaces (US, UK, DE, FR, IT, ES, JP) ([AMZPrep](https://amzprep.com/amazon-merch-on-demand/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/08/amazon-now-lets-you-design-custom-merch-using-ai/)). **No source shows Merch offering rugs or doormats**, and none confirms ceramic mugs. The custom rugs, doormats and mugs you see on Amazon come from the second route: a **Seller Central Professional account ($39.99/month) connected to a POD supplier** such as Printify, Printful or Gelato. The supplier prints and ships each order, you set the price, and Amazon takes a referral fee of about 15% for home and kitchen goods ([Printify help](https://help.printify.com/hc/en-us/articles/43556915343633-What-are-the-costs-associated-with-selling-on-Amazon); [Printful help](https://help.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/360014067159-What-are-Amazon-s-referral-fees)). Printify, Printful, Teelaunch and PrintKK all offer rugs and mats ([Printify rugs & mats](https://printify.com/app/products/home-and-living/rugs-and-mats); [Printful custom rugs](https://www.printful.com/custom-rugs); [PrintKK](https://www.printkk.com/custom-rugs)). In 2026, Printful, Printify and Gelato offer the smoothest native Amazon integrations, and all of them require the Professional plan ([PrintOnDemandBusiness](https://www.printondemandbusiness.com/blog/which-print-on-demand-companies-integrate-with-amazon-in-2025/)). Amazon Handmade prohibits POD outright ([Amazon Handmade](https://sell.amazon.com/programs/handmade)).

### Merch on Demand: gated, slow to scale, and newly tiered

Merch still requires an application. One blog estimates manual review at 2–8 weeks and 2026 approval rates at "around 30 to 40%", but Amazon publishes no such figures ([AMZPrep](https://amzprep.com/amazon-merch-on-demand/)). New accounts start at **10 design slots** and move up a ladder (25 → 100 → 500 → 1,000 → 2,000+) as they make sales. Reported thresholds are roughly 10 sales to reach Tier 25 and 100 sales to reach Tier 500 ([BeBold Digital](https://www.bebolddigital.com/blog/amazon-merch-tiers)). Listings with no sales in 18 months are now deleted automatically ([AMZPrep](https://amzprep.com/amazon-merch-on-demand/)). The defining 2026 change is royalty structure. Since June 1, sellers fall into three "royalty incentive groups" based on the share of sales driven by non-organic traffic (social, email, content or Sponsored Products ads, tracked with Amazon Attribution links) over a trailing 60 days. The **Creator** tier (organic only) pays 50% of the standard rate. **Plus** (≥15% non-organic) pays twice that. **Premium** (≥35%) pays 108% of Plus ([Merch Titans](https://merchtitans.com/blog/amazon-merch-royalty-changes-june-2026); [MyDesigns](https://mydesigns.io/blog/amazon-merch-royalty-changes-2026/)). On a $19.99 tee that works out to **$2.44 at Creator, $4.88 at Plus and about $5.27 at Premium** ([Pixels N' Profits](https://pixelsnprofits.com/amazon-merch-worth-it-2026/); [AMZPrep](https://amzprep.com/amazon-merch-on-demand/)). All of these figures come from third-party summaries, because Amazon's own announcement could not be retrieved. The direction is still unambiguous: Amazon now pays Merch sellers as marketers who bring outside traffic, not as designers who fill its search results.

Amazon has also become a competitor. In June 2026 it launched a free, US-only feature that lets shoppers prompt Alexa in the Shopping app to design custom tees, hoodies, polos, quarter-zips, tumblers and bottles, which Merch on Demand then prints ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/08/amazon-now-lets-you-design-custom-merch-using-ai/); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/amazon-wants-to-end-dodgy-knockoffs-with-its-own-ai-generated-custom-merch-printing?rand=100)). Any seller whose product is "a generic AI funny-cat shirt" now competes with the customer's own prompt.

### Seller Central + POD: full control, thin margins, real obligations

Seller Central opens the whole POD catalog, rugs included, but you carry the economics and account-health obligations of a real Amazon seller. One published example puts a Printful mug at $8.95 plus $3.99 or more for shipping ([Bootstrapping Ecommerce](https://bootstrappingecommerce.com/selling-on-amazon-with-printful)). Sold at $19.99, a 15% referral fee (about $3.00) leaves **roughly $4 gross per mug before the $39.99 monthly fee, ads and returns**. That calculation is illustrative, built from the cited figures. Printify's network is cheaper: about $3.68 base for a mug versus $7.56 at Printful ([podprofitcheck.org](https://podprofitcheck.org/)). Printify's catalog lists area rugs from about €15.16 and doormats from about €8.06 ([Printify rugs & mats](https://printify.com/app/products/home-and-living/rugs-and-mats); [Printify doormat](https://printify.com/app/products/1601/generic-brand/polyester-doormat/)). Rugs carry bulkier shipping and so need higher retail prices. The monthly fee means Seller Central loses money until you have a few proven designs, and POD production times put late-shipment and tracking metrics at risk. That makes it a second-stage channel for validated winners, not a place to test ideas.

### Etsy allows AI + POD, with three disclosures and a crowded buyer pool

Etsy is the most natural home for home goods and niche gifts sold through POD. It remains permitted under Etsy's "Designed by" seller role, which explicitly covers seller-prompted AI art produced by a third party ([Etsy Creativity Standards](https://www.etsy.com/legal/creativity); [Sarah Sewell summary](https://sarahsewell.substack.com/p/etsys-creativity-standards-update)). A compliant 2026 listing needs **"Designed by" attribution, disclosure of your POD provider as a production partner, and a statement of AI use** ([ShieldMyShop](https://www.shieldmyshop.com/blog/2026-05-21-etsy-production-partner-disclosure-pod-guide); [Artomate](https://www.artomate.app/blog/etsy-ai-disclosure-policy-2026)). Several blogs describe a January 14, 2026 policy update that added an AI checkbox, and a compliance vendor claims Etsy removed about 12,000 listings for missing AI disclosure in Q1 2026 ([iscompliant.app](https://iscompliant.app/Blog/etsy-creativity-standards-pod-sellers-guide)). Neither claim could be verified against Etsy, but the direction matches Etsy's June 2025 tightening, which requires designs to originate from the seller ([getcoai](https://getcoai.com/news/etsy-balances-ai-and-authenticity-with-new-creativity-standards-for-sellers)).

Fees take roughly 12–20% of a sale: $0.20 per listing, 6.5% transaction, about 3% + $0.25 processing, and a 15% Offsite Ads fee on attributed sales. That fee is optional below $10k/year in sales and 12% and mandatory above it ([Craftybase](https://craftybase.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-etsy-fees)). Printify's tee base cost is about $9.04 ([podprofitcheck.org](https://podprofitcheck.org/)). A $25 tee therefore keeps about $13 (roughly 52%) before shipping and ads, falling to about 37% when an Offsite Ads sale is charged. That is far better per unit than Merch's $2.44. The market itself is healthy but crowded. **Q2 2026 GMS was $2.6B, up 7.5% year on year, but active buyers were flat at about 87M while active sellers grew 5.9% to about 5.7M** ([Etsy Q2 2026 shareholder letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001370637/000137063726000079/q226shareholderletter.htm)). Each seller is competing for a shrinking share of attention.

### The royalty marketplaces have mostly closed to newcomers

The "upload once, earn royalties everywhere" model has decayed. Since September 2025, Redbubble charges Standard-tier artists a platform fee of up to 50% of monthly earnings, capped at $150 ([Redbubble Help](https://help.redbubble.com/hc/en-us/articles/50959863016724-How-does-my-Account-Tier-determine-my-account-fees)). Society6 became invite-only in 2025 and pays a fixed 5–10% ([ecommerceceo](https://www.ecommerceceo.com/best-society6-alternatives/)). Zazzle restructured its payouts around referral links ([Zazzle Help](https://www.zazzle.co.nz/hc/article/ambassador_referral_commissions_creator_royalties-30810357489175)). **Spring (formerly Teespring) stopped taking orders on September 15, 2026** ([Amaze Commerce](https://www.amazecommerce.com/spring-update)). TeePublic is the exception, with flat royalties of $3–4 per tee at full price and $1–2 during sales ([AutoKeyWorder](https://autokeyworder.com/blog/teepublic-vs-redbubble/)). The structural lesson is that a newcomer should own the storefront and the pricing (Etsy, Shopify or TikTok Shop, all of which Printify, Printful and Gelato integrate with), not rent space on a royalty platform whose terms keep worsening ([Gelato integrations](https://www.gelato.com/integrations)).

## Claude is the brain and the plumbing, never the painter

Claude's role in a POD pipeline is narrower than YouTube thumbnails suggest, and more useful. **Claude has vision input but no native image generation.** Opus 4.7 reads images but does not output them, and visual output comes through code: SVG, HTML/canvas, and the Claude Design product launched in April 2026 for prototypes, decks and marketing assets ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/17/anthropic-launches-claude-design-a-new-product-for-creating-quick-visuals/); [suprmind.ai](https://suprmind.ai/hub/claude/)). Across a full pipeline, Claude's job divides into four stages.

At the **research stage**, Claude analyzes best-sellers and finds recurring niches, phrases, palettes and fonts. One creator fed 250 Merch designs from Merch Informer into Claude to surface repeated niches ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TtKtenCiHkI)). Others use Claude as a "POD design research expert" that returns around 100 design prompts ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=956rB728VJk)). Claude in Chrome, generally available on paid plans since August 2026, can browse competitor listings for you ([Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260827-claude-chrome-available/)).

At the **design stage**, Claude writes prompts for image models and critiques their output. It can also write finished designs directly as SVG. That covers typographic slogan tees, geometric patterns and simple icons, which are a large share of best-sellers, and Printful and Printify both accept SVG uploads ([svgvector](https://www.svgvector.com/blog/svg-for-tshirts-guide.html)).

At the **listing stage**, Claude writes titles, descriptions and Etsy's 13 tags. At the **operations stage**, Claude Code connects to community MCP servers and skills: Printify MCP servers that create, upload and publish products ([Glama](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/aaravbedi04-stack/printify-mcp)), a 17-tool Printful MCP ([GitHub](https://github.com/Purple-Horizons/printful-mcp)), and an "etsy-automate" skill that runs niche research through to draft listings via Etsy API v3 ([GitHub](https://github.com/gab-spie/etsy-automate)). These projects are unofficial and unaudited, so check their code before giving one your API token.

For art Claude cannot draw, the 2026 toolkit has settled around a few tools. Ideogram leads on text in images, Recraft offers native vector and transparent PNG export, Midjourney has the highest artistic quality, and **Adobe Firefly is the only one offering formal IP indemnification** ([nesyona/PickAI](https://nesyona.com/articles/best-ai-image-generators-print-on-demand-2026)). Licensing is the trap. **Midjourney's free trial is licensed CC BY-NC (non-commercial)** and Recraft keeps the rights to free-plan images, so neither free tier works for POD. Recraft Pro ($10/month) and Midjourney's paid plans grant commercial rights ([terms.law](https://terms.law/ai-output-rights/midjourney/); [Rangy](https://rangy.ai/blog/ai-image-generators-2026-complete-guide/)). Print templates are large: Merch's tote template, for example, is 4500×5400 px ([Merch Informer](https://merchinformer.com/merch-amazon-listings/)). That is another reason vector output or an upscaler belongs in the pipeline.

Technically, the "fully autonomous Etsy empire" pitched on Medium is easy to build ([Medium](https://medium.com/@charles-ross/how-im-using-claude-ai-to-build-a-fully-autonomous-etsy-empire-and-you-can-steal-the-entire-bd9bebc3dd1c)). It is also exactly the catalog that Etsy's originality rules, Amazon's trademark flagging and Anthropic's spam prohibition are aimed at. The defensible design is automation up to drafts, with a human approving every design and listing.

## IP risk runs one way: you can't protect it but can be sued

AI-assisted POD sellers face an asymmetric legal position. On the protection side, **purely AI-generated designs are not copyrightable in the US**. The Copyright Office's January 2025 report concluded that "prompts essentially function as instructions that convey unprotectable ideas," although human selection, arrangement and modification can be protected. The Supreme Court declined *Thaler v. Perlmutter* on March 2, 2026, leaving the human-authorship requirement in place ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/supreme-court-denies-review-in-ai-authorship-case); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=1c80f0d3-c6b5-4b2d-af10-77baa816751d)). Anthropic assigns you its rights in outputs "if any," and that qualifier matters, because a competitor can legally copy your raw AI design ([Consumer Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)). Real editing in a vector tool, or SVGs shaped by specific creative choices, strengthens your position without guaranteeing registration.

On the liability side, you carry the full risk. Merch terminations are described as permanent, with listings, reviews and sales all lost, and designs are "getting flagged for trademark issues that would've passed two years ago" ([Merch Titans](https://merchtitans.com/blog/how-to-avoid-trademark-infringement-amazon-merch-on-demand); [AMZ Sellers Attorney](https://www.amazonsellers.attorney/blog/navigating-amazon-merch-on-demand-account-termination-and-dmca-disputes)). Across platforms the usual escalation runs from DMCA takedown to listing removal to frozen payouts to a wiped catalog ([Printify](https://printify.com/knowledge-hub/copyright-vs-trademark-print-on-demand/)). Language models can reproduce trademarked catchphrases without flagging them, so **every Claude-suggested slogan needs a USPTO clearance check** before upload. Tools such as Trademark Bulk (built around apparel Class 025), Niche Hunter Pro and Merch Informer speed this up ([Trademark Bulk](https://www.producthunt.com/products/trademark-bulk); [Niche Hunter Pro](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/bfabjhghkpjofoajcpnnbmhjajoloebm)). Amazon's own AI tool automatically rejects designs that imply ties to sports teams or franchises ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/08/amazon-now-lets-you-design-custom-merch-using-ai/)), which shows where enforcement is heading. On AI disclosure, Etsy requires it. For Merch, no confirmed disclosure field was found, and the one source claiming Merch requires disclosure sells metadata-stripping tools ([RemoveAILabel](https://removeailabel.com/blog/merch-by-amazon-ai-generated-designs-policy)). The safe rule is to never strip provenance and to disclose wherever a field exists. Anthropic's Usage Policy separately forbids IP infringement and passing off AI-assisted work "without proper permission or attribution" ([Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)).

## The YouTube numbers mostly measure teaching, not selling

Most "make money with Claude" videos fall into a few genres: faceless channels scripted with Claude, "I made $X with Claude Code" build-in-public stories, service businesses built on Claude, and POD pipelines where Claude does the research and SEO. The best-documented headline claim is "How to make $500,000 with Claude Code in 2026." By the creator's own breakdown, over $300K came from a SaaS product and **about $100K from YouTube ads and sponsorships, which is money from making content about Claude Code** ([videohighlight](https://videohighlight.com/v/TLBZ6sCkqaU)). "How to Make $10k/Month with Claude AI" is itself framed as a course ([videohighlight](https://videohighlight.com/v/iiU0tPB2-Yg)). The pattern is clearest among AI-automation-agency educators. Liam Ottley's reported coaching revenue ($150–200K/month) exceeds his agency revenue ($110–120K/month) ([automationatlas](https://automationatlas.io/creators/liam-ottley/)), and Nick Saraev's paid Skool group reportedly earns about $300K/month ([automationatlas](https://automationatlas.io/creators/nick-saraev/)). The faceless-channel claims of "$48,700/month" and "$50,000/month" circulate mainly on SEO mirror sites with no verification ([Growth-a-Roo](https://thegrowtharoalert.beehiiv.com/p/build-a-48k-mo-faceless-channel-by-next-month)). No video found in this research makes a verified "I made $X with Claude + Amazon POD" claim with evidence.

The POD videos show real workflows. One tutorial walks through a six-step Etsy pipeline with a Claude "reverse-prompt research agent" and SEO generator ([videohighlight](https://videohighlight.com/v/U96IIstNHl0)). Others cover AI tumblers ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Kz5W11_4Mg)) and Printify rugs on TikTok ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/printify-rugs-review)). What these videos leave out is the 2025–26 counter-evidence. Amazon's Merch royalty cut and its shopper-facing AI tool both postdate many of the tutorials. YouTube has demonetized "inauthentic," mass-produced content since July 15, 2025 ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). WIRED-sourced reporting documents human Etsy sellers losing 30–98% of sales to AI listings ([Jeweller Magazine](https://www.jewellermagazine.com/Article/15316/Etsy-facing-long-term-headache-because-of-AI)). The **FTC** has permanently banned or sued several "AI-powered" e-commerce income schemes. These include FBA Machine / Passive Scaling (over $15M taken), Ascend Ecom (at least $25M in consumer losses) and Air AI, under an enforcement sweep that has continued into the new administration ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/08/ftc-case-against-e-commerce-business-opportunity-scheme-its-operators-results-permanent-ban-industry); [Benesch Law](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/one-year-in-ftcs-operation-ai-comply-continues-under-new-administration-signaling-enduring-enforcement-focus/)). Those cases target done-for-you store sellers, not tutorial creators. They still show that "AI + e-commerce + passive income" is the pitch regulators treat as a red flag.

The honest earnings picture for POD is modest. Blogs cite **$50–300/month for beginners and $1–2k+/month for consistent sellers** ([Artomate](https://www.artomate.app/blog/etsy-print-on-demand-side-hustle-2026-earnings-how-to-start)), and "serious" Merch sellers at $500–5,000+/month ([Pixels N' Profits](https://pixelsnprofits.com/amazon-merch-worth-it-2026/)). No rigorous survey exists, and survivorship bias inflates every one of these figures. The outliers are real but narrow. The best-known is a racquet-sports niche seller reported to have made over $3.2M from one product line ([TrueProfit](https://trueprofit.io/blog/is-print-on-demand-profitable)). That example shows what does work: deep knowledge of one niche, not volume. With AI making designs nearly free to produce, volume is no longer an advantage. The advantages that remain are niche insight, external traffic and compliance.

## Services and software beat products on expected value

Outside POD, the same principle applies: Claude pays best where a human sells an outcome. The strongest market signal is in freelancing. **Fiverr's Q2 2026 revenue fell 10% year on year** as generative AI eroded low-value gigs, while gross order volume for large programming projects rose 34% ([SelfEmployed.com](https://www.selfemployed.com/news/fiverr-q2-2026-earnings-ai-freelance/); [Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/fiverr-q2-revenue-slips-10-amid-ai-led-market-shift)). On Upwork, demand for AI integration rose 178% and for chatbot development 71% ([AI Magazine / Upwork](https://aimagazine.com/globenewswire/3232122)), while writing projects fell about 32% ([SkillScouter](https://skillscouter.com/freelance-statistics/)). A Claude Code user who sells finished software, integrations and automations at fixed prices is on the growing side of that split. One who sells AI copywriting is on the shrinking side. AI automation services for small businesses are commonly quoted at **$1.5k–5k setup plus $300–1.5k/month retainers** ([Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/how-to-start-an-ai-automation-agency)). Recurring retainers give them the best ceiling-to-risk ratio of anything reviewed here, but client acquisition decides success, and the course industry around them is, in Reddit's verdict, where "the grift" lives ([Ciela AI](https://ciela.ai/blogs/ai-automation-agency-scam-reddit)).

Micro-SaaS built with Claude Code has an unbounded ceiling and a brutal median. Across 5,079 Stripe-verified startups the **median was $169/month**, and AI projects came in at $156 ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-analyzed-5-079-stripe-verified-startups-f0f6bd053f)). Only 7.6% of apps on vibe-coding platform subdomains report any revenue at all, although products sold *to* vibe coders show revenue half the time ([BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/how-to-make-money-vibe-coding)). KDP permits AI content but asks three disclosure questions and caps uploads at 3 titles per day. Low-content books earn about $1–3 per sale in heavily saturated categories ([ScribeCount](https://scribecount.com/author-resource/protecting-books/kdp-ai-content-policy-the-compliance-guide); [Vappingo](https://www.vappingo.com/word-blog/?p=12351)). Digital products such as Notion systems, spreadsheets and printables cost almost nothing to start, and generic AI versions are oversaturated for the same reason. Etsy's disclosure rules apply to them too ([AIMetadataCleaner](https://aimetadatacleaner.com/blog/etsy-ai-disclosure-metadata-guide-2026)). Faceless content has to get past YouTube's inauthentic-content rules, which reportedly wiped 16 AI channels with 35M combined subscribers ([logie.ai](https://logie.ai/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026-monetization/)). Selling Claude skills and MCP servers has distribution through the Claude Code plugin marketplace launched in April 2026, but skills are shared mostly for free and easy to copy. The workable model is a hosted, usage-priced MCP/API, which is really SaaS ([Agent37](https://www.agent37.com/blog/mcp-skills-monetization)). Anthropic has no cash affiliate program. Guest Passes give 7-day trials, and at most a small amount of credit ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13456702-claude-code-and-cowork-guest-passes)).

## Anthropic lets you sell outputs, not your account

On the terms, your position is clearer than most videos imply. Pro and Max run under the **Consumer Terms (effective October 8, 2025)**, which assign you Anthropic's rights in outputs "if any" and do not restrict commercial use of what you make ([Consumer Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)). Using Pro interactively, or running Claude Code on your own machine, to produce designs, listings, code or client deliverables that you sell is permitted. Five things are not. First, "automated or non-human means" of accessing the service other than an API key or a use Anthropic explicitly permits. Second, sharing your login or making your account available to anyone else. Third, reselling the Services or building competing models. Fourth, since the February 2026 clarification, **routing Free/Pro/Max credentials through third-party tools or products that serve other users**: "developers building products… should use API key authentication," and advertised limits "assume ordinary, individual usage of Claude Code and the Agent SDK" ([Claude Code legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/2/anthropic-officially-bans-using-subscription-authentication-for-third-party-claude-use)). Fifth, the Usage Policy's bans on spam, fake reviews, plagiarism, deceptive practices and IP infringement, plus required human review and AI disclosure in high-risk domains such as legal and financial advice ([Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)).

For POD, this means a Claude Code session on your laptop using a Printify MCP to draft products for your own shop is ordinary use of a native Anthropic app. A cron-driven, always-on bulk-listing bot sits closer to the line. It also runs into spam rules at both Anthropic and the marketplaces. For services, you deliver the code and the client owns it. If the client's app calls Claude at runtime, it needs its own API key under the Commercial Terms, billed separately, and never your subscription. Consumer plans come with **no IP indemnity**, so do not promise clients originality warranties you cannot back. On plan choice, Pro at $20/month ($17 billed annually) includes Claude Code, Cowork, Claude in Chrome and Claude Design, which is ample for a POD shop. Chat and Code share one usage pool, so sustained daily agentic coding for clients realistically needs Max at $100 or $200/month ([claude.com/pricing](https://claude.com/pricing); [support.claude.com](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11145838-using-claude-code-with-your-pro-or-max-plan)).

## Where to start: one niche, one shop, outside traffic

If POD is the goal, begin on **Etsy with Printify**, not Amazon. Etsy needs no application, pays about five times Merch's organic royalty per tee, and supports rugs, mugs and tumblers from day one. Pick one niche where you have genuine insight, such as a profession, hobby, sport or community you belong to, because niche knowledge is the one input AI has not made free.

Use Claude Pro for the full back office. It can mine competitor listings with Claude in Chrome, generate 50 phrase and concept candidates, write typographic designs directly as SVG, write prompts for a paid image tool (Recraft Pro at $10/month or Ideogram for text-heavy art), and draft titles, descriptions and 13 tags. Then personally edit and approve roughly 20–30 designs. Run every phrase through a USPTO check, and set "Designed by", production-partner and AI disclosures on every listing. Order samples of your two or three best products before scaling, since Printify's provider quality varies. Build external traffic from the first week through Pinterest, TikTok or a relevant community. External traffic is what separates Etsy shops that sell from ones that don't, and it doubles your Merch royalty later.

Apply to Merch on Demand the same week, because approval can take up to two months. Once you are approved, upload only designs already proven on Etsy, and link to them with Amazon Attribution tags to aim for the Plus tier. Open Seller Central ($39.99/month) for rugs and mugs only after specific designs have sold repeatedly. Total cash outlay beyond Claude stays under about $50 for the first month. Treat 90 days and your first 10 organic sales as the go/no-go test.

If you can code or are comfortable selling to businesses, run a parallel track that will probably pay more. Offer fixed-price Claude Code builds (automations, integrations, small web apps) to local businesses or on Upwork, where demand is growing rather than collapsing.

## Conclusion

Generative AI has driven the cost of producing designs toward zero, and every platform has responded by shifting value toward what AI cannot supply. Amazon now pays for traffic rather than uploads, Etsy for originality and disclosure, and freelance markets for outcomes rather than words. A Claude subscription is best understood as a way to get a small team's research, copywriting, coding and operations work for $20–200 a month. It pays off when you supply the scarce inputs: taste, niche knowledge, audience and accountability. Anyone who promises passive income from bulk AI output is selling the one Claude business that reliably works, which is teaching other people to try it.

Uncertainty remains significant. No audited data exists on median POD or Merch seller earnings. Most 2026 Amazon and Etsy policy details come from third-party summaries rather than the platforms themselves. Anthropic could still add image generation or change usage limits. Test cheaply, measure your own conversion rates, and scale only what sells. That is the safest way to act while the landscape is still shifting.
